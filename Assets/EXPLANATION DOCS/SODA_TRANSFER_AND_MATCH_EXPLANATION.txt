SODA TRANSFER AND MATCH SYSTEM
==============================

Scripts covered:
- Assets/Scripts/Main Scripts/Core Gameplay/UniversalSodaTransferSystem.cs
- Assets/Scripts/Main Scripts/Editor/TransferAlgorithmDiagnostics.cs

Related:
- Assets/Scripts/Main Scripts/Core Gameplay/Board.cs
- Assets/Scripts/Main Scripts/Core Gameplay/Box.cs


1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM DOES
========================

When the player drops a Box onto the board, the sodas inside the neighbouring
Boxes are allowed to move around so that same coloured sodas gather together.
When one Box ends up full of a single colour, that Box is "packed": it counts as
a match, it is removed from the board, and the player is rewarded.

The system is split into two halves on purpose:

UniversalSodaTransferSystem (MonoBehaviour)
    Owns timing, animation, and the safety of the actual soda objects.
    It never decides *what* should move.

TransferAlgorithm (plain static class, no Unity references)
    Owns the decision making. Given a snapshot of the Boxes, it answers one
    question: "what is the single next transfer?"

The split exists so the decision logic can be replayed and tested in the editor
without a scene, a prefab, or a frame of animation.


2. THE RESOLVE LOOP
===================

Board.ResolvePlacement starts UniversalSodaTransferSystem.Resolve for the Box
the player just dropped. Resolve then repeats this cycle:

1. Retire terminal Boxes.
   Any Box in the group that is now packed or now empty leaves the board first.

2. Snapshot the group.
   BuildStates turns every live Box into a BoxState: id, column, row, capacity,
   placement order, and a colour to count dictionary.
   BuildDirectEdges records which of those Boxes touch orthogonally.

3. Guard against loops.
   BuildSignature makes a short text fingerprint of the whole group. If the same
   fingerprint appears twice, the group is ping-ponging and the loop stops.

4. Ask TransferAlgorithm.TrySelectMove for one Decision.

5. Execute that Decision as a transaction.
   For each soda: reserve a slot in the target, remove from the source, accept
   into the target, animate, commit. Any failed step rolls back. That is what
   stops two sodas from landing in the same slot.

6. Repeat until no Decision is available.

The important property is that only ONE transfer is ever in flight. Everything
else in this system exists to choose that one transfer well.


3. THE TRANSFER RULE
====================

A soda may move from Box S to Box T only when all of these hold:

- S and T are directly adjacent (up, down, left, right, never diagonal).
- T already contains at least one soda of that colour.
- T has a free slot.
- S is not itself already packed.

The second rule is the one that shapes the whole puzzle: a colour can only
spread into Boxes that already show it. It is why a Box that fills up with two
different colours becomes a dead weight, and why the order of transfers matters
so much.


4. WHY A GREEDY CHOICE WAS NOT ENOUGH
=====================================

The original planner scored each possible next transfer on its own merits:
does it complete a Box, does it empty its source, does it concentrate a colour,
and so on. The best scoring transfer won.

That works until a Box becomes a bridge.

Consider three Boxes in a row, capacity 4, and the middle one is the Box the
player just dropped:

    left: 2 orange      middle: 1 orange      right: 1 orange + 1 blue

Four orange sodas exist in the group, so a four match is available. But every
route to it has to pass THROUGH the middle Box, because left and right do not
touch each other.

Greedy scoring rated "middle gives its orange to left" and "left gives its two
orange to middle" almost identically, and then broke the tie in favour of the
Box that was just placed. That emptied the bridge. With the middle Box empty,
the right Box had no orange neighbour left, the group fell apart, and the match
the player was aiming for never happened.

This is exactly the bug the player reported: "the middle soda goes into the left
or the right Box and the four match does not happen."


5. THE PLANNER
==============

TrySelectMove now runs TrySelectMatchPlanMove BEFORE the greedy scorer.

The rule it enforces is wider than "always take the four match":

    Gather one colour into one Box as far as the group allows.
    If that reaches a full Box, it is a match.
    If it does not, the sodas still end up together instead of scattered.

The second half matters as much as the first. Three sodas of a colour with no
fourth on the board are still worth gathering, because the next Box off the rail
may carry that fourth. Scattering them, or emptying the Box that bridges them,
strands that colour permanently.

TrySelectMatchPlanMove does a breadth first search over the group:

- Cheap reject first. CouldAnyColorConcentrate checks whether any colour even
  appears twice in the group. If not there is nothing to gather, and the search
  returns before allocating anything. Most placements exit here, which is what
  keeps the cost off the frame budget.

- GenerateLegalMoves lists EVERY legal transfer, in every direction, for every
  colour, and for every amount from the largest down to one.

- Each move is applied to a copy of the snapshot, fingerprinted, and queued.

- Every reached state is scored by GetConcentrationScore. The search keeps the
  first move of the path that reaches the best score it can find.

- A packed Box is the ceiling, so the moment one appears the search stops
  immediately and returns the first move of the path that got there.

Because the search walks breadth first, the path it finds is the shortest one.
Because it works on the adjacency graph rather than on coordinates, it behaves
identically for a row, a column, an elbow, or any other connected shape. There
is no special case for L shapes anywhere in the code.

If no move improves on where the group already stands, the old greedy scorer
runs unchanged. Nothing about the previous behaviour was thrown away; it simply
lost its right of first refusal.


5b. HOW A STATE IS SCORED
=========================

A pile is "live" while its Box still has room to reach capacity in that colour.
Three orange plus one blue in a four slot Box is NOT live: those sodas can never
become a match. Dead piles score nothing at all, so the planner is never tempted
to walk the board into the very state it exists to avoid.

GetConcentrationScore then ranks a snapshot on two levels, packed into one int:

Leading term - the largest live pile anywhere in the group.
    This is the goal itself. Gather one colour into one Box, and a pile that
    reaches capacity is a match. Nothing outranks it.

Trailing term - the sum, over each colour separately, of the square of that
colour's own largest live pile, plus one when the Box holding it is a single
clean colour.
    Squaring rewards gathering over spreading: four greens together beat four
    greens one per Box by a wide margin.
    Giving every colour its own entry is what stops a colour that is already
    well gathered from hiding progress elsewhere. Without it, a Box sitting on
    three purple would set the bar at three, and gathering three greens from
    scratch would not register as an improvement at all.
    The plus one is the tie break players expect: given two ways to gather the
    same sodas, gather them into the Box that is already one clean colour rather
    than into one carrying a passenger.

Why this fixes the bridge bug: emptying the bridge Box scores badly, because
once the bridge is gone the two halves of the colour can never reach each other
and no deeper state in that branch can score well either. The planner sees that
before it commits, which is precisely what per move scoring could not do.


6. WHY EVERY AMOUNT, NOT JUST THE LARGEST
=========================================

The greedy scorer always proposed the largest transfer it could make. That is
usually right, and it is occasionally fatal:

    left: 2 orange    middle: 1 orange + 1 blue    right: 1 orange

Moving both of left's orange sodas into the middle fills the middle Box to 3
orange plus 1 blue. It is now full, mixed, and permanently stuck, and the group
is dead even though four orange sodas were sitting right there.

The route that works is smaller and runs the other way:

    right gives its 1 orange to middle    -> middle holds 2 orange + 1 blue
    middle gives its 2 orange to left     -> left holds 4 orange, packed

The search can only find that route if partial amounts are on the table, so
GenerateLegalMoves emits them. They are listed largest first, which keeps the
shortest and most natural paths at the front of the queue.


7. HOW THE FIRST MOVE IS CHOSEN WHEN SEVERAL PATHS TIE
======================================================

Different opening moves can lead to equally short match paths. The search ranks
its opening moves with the same CandidateComparer the greedy scorer uses, so
when the look-ahead has a free choice it makes the same choice the old system
would have made. The look-ahead only overrides the old behaviour when the old
behaviour would have missed the match.

This keeps the game feeling the same everywhere except in the situations the
player complained about.


8. BOUNDS
=========

The search is deliberately fenced in so a large custom board cannot stall a
phone:

PackedMatchSearchBoxLimit (14)
    Groups larger than this skip the search entirely. The limit is deliberately
    generous: real levels routinely connect ten or eleven Boxes, and those are
    exactly the groups where a missed match hurts most. An earlier limit of
    eight was silently turning the planner off in ordinary play.

FullDepthSearchBoxCount (5) and ShallowSearchDepth (4)
    Up to five Boxes the group is searched as deep as it needs. Past that the
    depth is capped at four instead of the search being skipped, so the planner
    still catches the multi step paths greedy scoring misses without a
    combinatorial blow up.

PackedMatchSearchStateLimit (4096)
    The search stops after this many distinct states and falls back to greedy.

Every state visited is fingerprinted, so the same arrangement is never explored
twice no matter how many different move orders reach it.


8b. THE TRANSFER TRACE
======================

UniversalSodaTransferSystem has a traceTransfers toggle in the Inspector. With
it on, every resolution writes its full snapshot and each chosen move to the
console, tagged with which branch decided it:

    [TransferTrace] step 1 trigger #24 :: #13@c3r1 cap4 {Purplex2} | ...
    [TransferTrace] chose 13->24: 2 Purple via match-path

The "via" tag is the useful part. "match-path" means the planner chose the move;
"greedy" means the planner found no improvement and the old scorer decided. A
missed match showing "via greedy" points at the planner being skipped - which is
how the eight Box limit above was found. Leave the toggle off for normal play.


9. THE EDITOR TESTS
===================

TransferAlgorithmDiagnostics replays the resolver loop with no scene attached.

It runs automatically after every recompile, and it can be run by hand from:

    Tools > Coca Sorting > Tests > Run Active Transfer Tests

Those named scenarios cover four layouts - horizontal, vertical, and both elbow
orientations - against eight soda arrangements each. Five of them require a four
match, including the bridge case and the partial amount trap described above.
The other three have NO four match available and require the colour to end up
gathered anyway; those three are the ones that reproduce the second report from
the levels, where a lone soda on the bridge was handed to a neighbour and three
sodas of a colour were stranded for good.

A second menu item:

    Tools > Coca Sorting > Tests > Sweep Every Three Box Layout

walks EVERY three Box arrangement the board can produce with two colours - 6912
of them across the four layouts. For each one it asks
TransferAlgorithm.ExploreReachableOutcomes what is genuinely achievable, then
requires the resolver to achieve it: the four match when one exists, and the
best gathering when one does not. Any layout that falls short is printed with
its exact contents, so a regression names itself.

The sweep runs once per editor session and can be re-run from the menu at any
time. Bump the suffix on SweepSessionKey after changing the planner so the next
recompile re-runs it rather than trusting a result from the old algorithm.


10. THINGS TO BE CAREFUL WITH
=============================

- Do not add a second soda colour system. Everything here keys off
  Soda.SodaColor and Box.GetSodaColorCounts.

- Do not let the planner touch scene objects. If TransferAlgorithm ever needs a
  Transform, the editor tests stop working and the logic stops being replayable.

- Box capacity is discovered from the SodaPosition children of the prefab, not
  hardcoded to 4. The algorithm reads BoxState.Capacity, so a Box with a
  different slot count already works.

- If a level ever connects more Boxes than PackedMatchSearchBoxLimit allows, the
  planner goes quiet and the old greedy behaviour returns without any warning.
  Turn on traceTransfers and look for moves decided "via greedy" before assuming
  the planner is at fault for a missed match.
